**Canada insolvencies June 2026** reached **13,254 filings**, up **5.7% from May** and **11.5% compared with June 2025**, according to figures released by the Office of the Superintendent of Bankruptcy (OSB) on 10 August and reported by BNN Bloomberg on 12 August. The monthly number is the sharper signal in this release — the quarterly consumer total rose just 1.1% — and it suggests the second quarter finished considerably stronger than it began.

## Summary

- **13,254 total insolvency filings** in June 2026
- Up **5.7% month over month** and **11.5% year over year**
- Q2 consumer insolvencies of **37,523**, the most since 2009, but up only **1.1%** on Q1
- Twelve months to 30 June: **145,762 consumer filings**

## Canada Insolvencies June 2026: Why the Monthly Number Matters

Quarterly data smooths out the shape of a trend. Monthly data shows it.

| Measure | Figure | Change |
|---|---|---|
| June 2026 insolvencies | **13,254** | **+5.7% vs May** |
| June 2026 vs June 2025 | — | **+11.5%** |
| Q2 2026 consumer insolvencies | 37,523 | +1.1% vs Q1 |
| Q2 2026 vs Q2 2025 (consumer) | — | +6.9% |

The contrast is instructive. On a quarterly basis the Canada insolvencies June 2026 release looks like a plateau — barely a percentage point above the first quarter. On a monthly basis, June was running at nearly double the quarter's annual growth rate.

Both are accurate. The quarter as a whole was flat because April and May were softer; June was not. Whether that is noise or the start of a steeper leg will only be settled by the July and August figures.

## What Insolvency Data Can and Cannot Tell You

Insolvency filings are a **lagging** measure. By the time someone files, the financial pressure has usually been building for months or years — through missed payments, revolving balances and, often, additional borrowing to keep earlier debts current.

That has two consequences worth stating plainly:

1. A rise in filings describes stress that **already happened**. It is not a forecast.
2. The households represented in the June number mostly began struggling well before June.

Charles St-Arnaud, chief economist at Servus Credit Union, told BNN Bloomberg that "high household debt and stagnating purchasing power have put pressure on Canadians' finances in recent years," adding that insolvency rates "appear to be stabilizing near levels last seen in 2009."

## Five Early Warning Signs Behind Canada Insolvencies June 2026

Because filings lag the problem, the useful question is what comes earlier. These are the signals that typically show up months before a formal filing:

1. **Using credit for essentials** — groceries, fuel or utilities going onto a card as a matter of routine rather than convenience.
2. **Paying only the minimum** on one or more cards for several consecutive months.
3. **Borrowing to service borrowing** — a new advance used to cover an existing payment. This is the single clearest signal of a cycle forming.
4. **A returned pre-authorized payment.** Beyond the fees, it means the timing of your income and your obligations has stopped lining up.
5. **Sitting at or near the limit** on a card or line of credit with no realistic path to bringing it down.

Any two of these occurring together is a reasonable trigger for a free consultation — well before the point where a filing is the only route left.

## Background

The Canada insolvencies June 2026 figure sits inside a quarter that was the heaviest for consumer insolvency filings since 2009. Q2 2026 recorded 37,523 consumer filings — 8,600 bankruptcies and 28,923 consumer proposals — alongside 1,281 business insolvencies, for 39,104 filings in total.

Ontario led all provinces with 15,029 filings, up 10.0% year over year, while Quebec recorded 9,361, up 3.0%. Our earlier reporting on [household debt reaching $2.6 trillion](/news/canada-household-debt-2-6-trillion-2026) covered the borrowing side of the same story.

## Why This Matters

For most households the practical value of the Canada insolvencies June 2026 release is not the number itself but what it implies about timing. The cheaper, less damaging remedies are the ones that require you to still have some room to manoeuvre.

- A **consumer proposal** requires sustainable monthly payments over up to five years — it is available to people who still have budget capacity.
- An **extended payment plan** on payday borrowing is a legal right in Ontario after a third loan in 63 days, with no penalty for prepaying or exiting.
- **Non-profit credit counselling** and a **Licensed Insolvency Trustee's** first consultation both cost nothing.

The Canada insolvencies June 2026 data closed a quarter that looked flat in aggregate and considerably less flat at the end.
