The headline from the **canada housing market july 2026** figures is unremarkable: sales up half a percent. Underneath it sits something that has not happened in 20 months. The MLS Home Price Index rose month over month for the first time since **November 2024**, and it did so while listings fell and inventory hit its lowest level of the year.

![Canada housing market july 2026 — an advisor reviewing home price and listing data with a client](https://images.pexels.com/photos/3823488/pexels-photo-3823488.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

## Summary

The Canadian Real Estate Association released its July figures on **August 18, 2026**:

- Home sales **+0.5% m/m** — a **fourth consecutive** monthly gain
- Sales **−5.3%** compared with July 2025
- New listings **−1.6% m/m** — a **third consecutive** decline
- **MLS Home Price Index +0.1% m/m** — first increase since **November 2024**; still **−3.3% y/y**
- Sales-to-new-listings ratio **51.3%** (long-term average 54.7%)
- Months of inventory **4.7** — the **lowest of 2026**
- National average price **$674,819**, **+0.2% y/y**

## Canada housing market july 2026: the turn beneath the headline

CREA senior economist **Shaun Cathcart** pointed directly at this in the release, saying "the more interesting story over the last few months has been below the surface," with markets "generally moving back towards balance."

The mechanism is straightforward. Sales have risen four months in a row. New listings have fallen three months in a row. When demand ticks up while supply steps back, the ratio between them tightens — and it has, to **51.3%**, converging on the long-term average of **54.7%**.

Inventory tells the same canada housing market july 2026 story from a different angle. At **4.7 months**, national inventory is the lowest recorded this year and sits slightly below the roughly five-month long-term average.

Note the asymmetry. The demand side is improving only modestly — sales remain **5.3% below** their year-earlier level. The supply side is moving faster, with listings withdrawing for three straight months.

That distinction matters for anyone reading this as a recovery story. Prices stopped falling not primarily because buyers surged back, but because sellers stepped back.

## Background: 20 months of declines

The MLS Home Price Index is CREA's quality-adjusted measure of price movement, designed to track the value of a comparable home rather than shifting mix in what happens to sell. It had been declining since late 2024.

A single **+0.1%** month in the canada housing market july 2026 figures does not reverse that. The index remains **3.3% lower** than a year ago, and the national average price of **$674,819** is up just **0.2%** year over year — essentially flat in nominal terms and negative after inflation, with the national CPI at 3.0% in July.

Regionally, CREA noted that Saskatchewan, New Brunswick and Newfoundland and Labrador remain borderline sellers' markets, while most other provinces are converging toward their long-term averages.

CREA chair **Garry Bhaura** framed the outlook cautiously, saying "more moderate housing market conditions can be expected to continue to bring buyers off the sidelines going forward."

## Why this matters

For households, the practical implications of the canada housing market july 2026 data cut in two directions.

**If you are saving to buy,** the window of falling prices appears to be narrowing. Inventory at its lowest level of the year and a tightening sales-to-listings ratio both reduce buyer leverage. That is a change in conditions, not yet a change in affordability — prices are still down year over year.

**If you own,** a first monthly price increase in 20 months is meaningful for equity, particularly for anyone whose renewal or refinancing depends on an appraised value. Still, one month is one month.

**If you are budgeting,** note what shelter costs did nationally: Statistics Canada reported shelter inflation of just **1.3%** year over year in July, well below the 3.0% all-items rate. Housing was not the main source of price pressure last month — fuel was.

## Canada housing market july 2026: key takeaways

1. **The MLS Home Price Index rose for the first time since November 2024**, up 0.1% month over month.
2. **Supply is tightening faster than demand is recovering** — listings down three months running, sales still 5.3% below last year.
3. **Inventory at 4.7 months is the lowest of 2026.**
4. **Prices remain down 3.3% year over year**; the average price of $674,819 is up just 0.2%.
5. **The next CREA report lands September 15, 2026.**

## Conclusion

July did not deliver a housing rebound. It delivered something quieter and arguably more significant: the first month in nearly two years in which the national price index did not fall.

Whether that becomes a trend depends largely on whether listings keep withdrawing. One month of data establishes a possibility, not a direction — and the September 15 release is the next real test.
