**Bank of Canada deliberations 2026** published on July 29 reveal a Governing Council that agrees on where the economy is now and disagrees on where it goes next. The summary, covering the discussion behind the July 15 decision to hold the policy rate at **2¼%**, records broad confidence that growth is rebounding and inflation easing — alongside "a range of views" on whether that rebound survives past the second half of 2026. For households, the split matters more than the hold.

| Topic | What Governing Council concluded |

| Agreement | Growth rebounding, inflation easing, reducing the policy trade-off |

## Where the Bank of Canada deliberations 2026 record shows agreement

Members were aligned on the near-term direction. Growth was rebounding and inflation was easing, which together **reduced the policy trade-off** the Council had been managing — the tension between supporting a weak economy and containing price pressures.

On inflation specifically, members projected a path to **about 2½% in the second half of 2026**, before reaching the 2% target in early 2027. Two conditions underpin that: oil prices declining as expected, and continued **excess supply and moderate wage growth** holding down domestic price pressure.

That second condition was reinforced the following day. Statistics Canada reported average weekly earnings of $1,337.77 in May 2026, up 3.4% year over year — firm, but not the kind of acceleration that would unsettle the Bank's forecast.

## Where members differed

The split is the news. While members expressed confidence in the near-term rebound, the Bank of Canada deliberations 2026 record shows **a range of views regarding sustainability beyond the second half of 2026**.

Several risks sit behind that divergence:

- **Trade.** Businesses reported adapting to the new environment and fewer cited trade uncertainty. But tariff-affected sectors remain below pre-conflict export levels, and **USMCA annual reviews** were flagged as a continuing downside risk.
- **Housing.** Weak but recovering, with members identifying the risk that recovery **stalls if affordability challenges persist**.
- **Consumers.** Resilience was described as uncertain if labour market conditions fail to improve — a pointed caveat given job vacancies have now been flat for five consecutive months at 495,700.
- **Oil.** The Council committed to "look through" direct oil price effects while stating it "would not let higher oil prices lead to persistent inflation."

## Background and timeline

The decision recorded in the Bank of Canada deliberations 2026 summary was the sixth consecutive hold. The economy had effectively stalled, with no GDP growth between the first quarter of 2025 and the first quarter of 2026, before indicators pointed to recovery in the second quarter and growth broadening beyond consumer and government spending.

## Why this matters for borrowers

A rate hold is not a neutral event; it is a decision that borrowing costs stay where they are. The **prime rate remains at 4.45%**, which anchors variable mortgages, home equity lines of credit and many personal loans.

What the Bank of Canada deliberations 2026 summary adds is a warning about planning horizons. The Council's own members cannot agree on conditions beyond this year. Any household budget that assumes rate cuts arriving on a schedule is building on a forecast the rate-setters themselves are divided about.

Two clarifications worth stating plainly:

- **Payday loan costs are not rate-linked.** They are capped federally at **$14 per $100** advanced, regardless of what the Bank does. A hold, a cut or a hike changes nothing about that price.
- **Instalment and prime-linked credit are rate-linked.** Here the hold genuinely matters, and a divided Council is a reason to stress-test a renewal or a variable payment rather than assume relief.

## Key takeaways

- The Bank of Canada deliberations 2026 summary confirms the policy rate held at **2¼%**.
- Members **agreed** growth is rebounding and inflation easing.
- Members **split** on whether the rebound lasts beyond the second half of 2026.
- Inflation is projected at **about 2½% in H2 2026**, hitting 2% in early 2027.
- Risks named: USMCA reviews, housing affordability, an unimproving labour market and oil prices.

## Conclusion

The headline from the Bank of Canada deliberations 2026 release is not the hold — that was known on July 15. It is that the people setting Canadian interest rates share a view of the present and not of the future.
