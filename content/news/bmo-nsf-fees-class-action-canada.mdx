The **BMO NSF fees class action** is now the odd one out. Four of Canada's five largest banks have settled near-identical claims over duplicate non-sufficient-funds charges for a combined **$43.4 million** — and Bank of Montreal has not. Its case, filed almost four years ago, **has still not been certified**, which means there is no settlement fund, no eligibility list and no payment date for BMO customers to watch.

*Published August 4, 2026. General information, not legal advice. Case status reflects publicly available information from class counsel.*

![Reviewing bank account records connected to the BMO NSF fees class action](https://images.pexels.com/photos/4482896/pexels-photo-4482896.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

## What the BMO NSF fees class action alleges

The BMO NSF fees class action concerns a billing sequence rather than a headline fee.

A biller presents a pre-authorized debit, the account is short, the payment fails, and an NSF fee is charged. The biller's system then presents **the same payment again** days later. It fails again, and a second fee follows.

The action, brought by Toronto firm Koskie Minsky LLP, alleges BMO charged **multiple NSF fees on a single attempted transaction** through vendor reprocessing, potentially breaching its account contracts and consumer protection legislation. The allegations have not been proven in court.

## Where the BMO NSF fees class action actually stands

| Milestone | Status |
|---|---|
| Statement of Claim issued | **August 30, 2022** |
| Case management assigned | Justice Akbarali, **October 5, 2022** |
| **Certification** | **Not yet granted** |
| Settlement | **None** |
| Class period | Not publicly defined |
| Payment date | **None** |

That certification line is the whole story. It is the gate every Canadian class action has to pass through before anything else can happen.

## Why certification matters more than the headline

Certification is a court's decision that a case is **appropriate to run as a class proceeding** — that there is an identifiable class, common issues, and that a class action is the preferable procedure. It is not a ruling that the claim is right.

Until a case is certified:

- **No settlement fund exists.** There is no money set aside and nothing to distribute.
- **No class period is fixed.** Nobody can say which dates would qualify.
- **No notice is sent.** Formal notice to class members follows certification.
- **No opt-out window opens.** That right arises before any settlement or judgment, not now.

This is why customers searching for a BMO NSF fees class action payout amount find nothing. There is nothing to find, and any site quoting a BMO figure is inventing it.

## How the other four resolved

The contrast is what makes BMO's position notable.

| Bank | Amount | Per person | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| TD | $15.9M | ~$88 | Approved **Feb 15, 2024** — concluded |
| Scotiabank | $10.45M | ~$42.82 | Approved **Jun 12, 2026** — paying out now |
| CIBC | $10M (proposed) | Not published | Approval hearing **Oct 19, 2026** |
| RBC | $7.05M | ~$25 | Approved **Sep 16, 2025** — concluded |
| **BMO** | **—** | **—** | **Not certified** |

Two are finished and paid. Scotiabank's money began moving in **July 2026**, notified through its app rather than by letter. CIBC's proposal awaits a judge in October.

**None of the four admitted liability.** Settling without admission is standard practice in Canadian class actions and says nothing definitive about the merits — which is also why BMO's decision not to settle cannot be read as an admission of anything either.

## What to do about the BMO NSF fees class action

**Do nothing that costs money.** There is no registration, no filing fee and no claim form for an uncertified action. Class counsel state plainly that anyone meeting the class definition is automatically included and that no registration is required.

**Do keep records.** If you had repeated NSF charges on the same biller between roughly 2020 and 2024, keep those statements. Should the case be certified and later settle, eligibility would be determined from bank records, but your own copies cost nothing to retain.

**Do contact class counsel with questions,** not your branch. Koskie Minsky administers the action.

**Do not expect a timeline.** Certification motions in Canadian class actions routinely take years, and this claim is already approaching four years old without one.

**Do not pay anyone offering to "register" you.** Settlement-themed fraud follows every publicised case. In 2025 the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre recorded more than **112,000 reports** and over **$704 million** in reported losses, a record year.

## The change that already happened

While these five cases have wound through the courts, the underlying practice was addressed by regulation.

Since **March 12, 2026**, amendments to the Financial Consumer Protection Framework Regulations cap NSF fees at **$10** at federally regulated banks, down from the **$45 to $48** the Big Six charged — a weighted average of about **$46.85**. The rules also prohibit **more than one NSF fee within two business days** on the same account, and bar any fee where the overdraft is under **$10**.

That second provision is the direct answer to the re-presentment sequence at the heart of every one of these lawsuits. Whatever happens to the BMO NSF fees class action, the conduct it complains about cannot be repeated in the same way.

## Key takeaways

- The BMO NSF fees class action was filed **August 30, 2022** and **remains uncertified**.
- There is **no settlement, no class period and no payout date** for BMO customers.
- Four rival banks settled for **$43.4 million** combined; **none admitted liability**.
- Certification is the gate — nothing moves until a court grants it.
- **No registration is required**, and no legitimate party charges a fee.
- Since **March 12, 2026**, NSF fees are capped at **$10**, once per two business days.

## The bottom line

For BMO customers, the honest position is that this remains an unresolved lawsuit rather than a pending refund. The four settled cases suggest a plausible range of roughly $25 to $88 per person if it ever gets there — but certification has not happened, and it may take years or not happen at all.

The concrete improvement arrived in March, and it applies to every federally regulated bank including BMO: a bounced payment now costs $10, and it cannot be charged twice inside two business days.
