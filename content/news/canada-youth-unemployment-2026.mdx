Canada youth unemployment 2026 sat at **12.6%** in July and did not move, even as the national rate fell to a two-year low of **6.4%** and employers added **75,000** jobs. The month's gains went overwhelmingly to workers aged 25 to 54. For under-25s, a strong jobs report changed nothing.

## Canada youth unemployment 2026: the age gap

Statistics Canada's Labour Force Survey covers the reference week of **July 12 to 18, 2026**.

| Group | Unemployment rate | July movement |
|---|---|---|
| **Youth (15–24)** | **12.6%** | Unchanged |
| Core-aged men (25–54) | 5.8% | — |
| Core-aged women (25–54) | 5.2% | — |
| Age 55+ | 5.2% | — |
| **National** | **6.4%** | −0.1 pt |

Core-aged workers aged 25 to 54 added **51,000 jobs (+0.4%)** in July — the bulk of the national increase.

## What Canada youth unemployment 2026 actually shows

The headline improvement was real: unemployment is down **0.5 percentage points year over year**, employment rose 0.4%, and the employment rate ticked up to 60.9%.

But the July gains were **concentrated by age**, not spread across it. Core-aged employment rose by 51,000. The youth rate held flat at 12.6%. On the month's evidence, the labour market tightened for people already established in it and stayed where it was for people trying to get in.

> At 12.6%, a young Canadian looking for work faces a jobless rate **roughly 2.2 to 2.4 times** that of every other age group in the survey.

Some of that gap is structural and always present. Young workers are disproportionately new entrants, students, seasonal and part-time staff, and employees in high-turnover industries. **A youth rate above the national average is normal in every year.** What is worth noting is that it did not improve in a month when almost everything else did.

## The financial consequence nobody reports

Here is the part of Canada youth unemployment 2026 that matters beyond the labour statistics, and it compounds quietly.

Entry into stable employment is also **entry into the credit system**. A Canadian who begins full-time work at 24 instead of 21 does not just lose three years of earnings — they start building a credit file three years later. Since Canadian credit scores are built on length of history as much as on payment behaviour, that delay follows them well past the point they find work.

The practical chain looks like this:

1. **Delayed first job** → no stable income to underwrite.
2. **Delayed first credit product** → no tradeline reporting to Equifax or TransUnion.
3. **Thin or absent file at 25** → declined for mainstream products at exactly the age most people need a car or a lease.
4. **Higher pricing when approved** → because the file is short, not because the borrower is risky.

None of that appears in a jobs report. It shows up years later as a 26-year-old with good income being declined for a first loan.

## Canada youth unemployment 2026 and the wage picture

Average hourly wages across all employees rose **2.8% year over year to $37.17**. Set against the most recent inflation reading — the **June 2026 CPI at 2.8%** — pay is running level with prices rather than ahead of them. Note the months differ; July's CPI is not published until later in August.

For young workers, that combination is the difficult one: **harder to get hired, and no real-terms pay improvement once hired.**

## What young Canadians can do in the meantime

If the job market is slow to open, the credit clock does not have to stay stopped. Three things build a Canadian credit file without requiring a full-time job.

- **Rent reporting.** Rent paid to a landlord is invisible to Equifax and TransUnion. Routed through a reporting service it becomes a tradeline — and several services back-report up to 24 months already paid.
- **A secured credit card.** A refundable deposit, one small recurring bill on it, paid in full. This is a near-certain approval and it starts the revolving history scoring models weight most.
- **A deposit account with income routed into it.** Even part-time income creates the bank-transaction pattern lenders read when there is no score to read.

Why this matters specifically against Canada youth unemployment 2026: credit scores are built on **length** of history as much as on behaviour. Someone who starts the clock at 20 on a $5-a-month rent tradeline arrives at 25 with five years of file, regardless of when full-time work began. Someone who waits for the job to start first arrives with nothing.

## Key takeaways

- **Youth unemployment held at 12.6%** in July 2026 — unchanged, while the national rate fell.
- **Core-aged workers (25–54) added 51,000 jobs**, driving most of the national gain.
- **The youth rate is 2.2–2.4× every other age group** (5.8% men, 5.2% women, 5.2% for 55+).
- **National unemployment fell to 6.4%**, a two-year low, on employment up 75,000.
- **Wages rose 2.8% to $37.17/hour**, level with the latest inflation figure.

## The bottom line

Canada youth unemployment 2026 is the exception inside an otherwise strong report. Employment rose, unemployment hit a two-year low, and the improvement passed under-25s by entirely — their rate did not move.

The consequence runs further than a single month of job searching. Late entry into work means late entry into credit, and a thin file at 25 shapes borrowing costs for years afterwards. That is the part a 12.6% figure does not convey, and it is the reason this number deserves more attention than it usually gets.
