Loan charge-off rates Canada watchers rarely see move this fast just did. In its Q2 2026 results released August 6, goeasy reported a **net charge-off rate of 16.7%** — up 800 basis points from **8.7%** a year earlier. Canada's largest listed non-prime lender is now writing off roughly one dollar in six of its loan book annually. The figure deserves careful reading, because it says something real about households and something misleading about timing.

## Loan charge-off rates Canada: what the numbers show

| Measure | Q2 2026 | Q2 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Net charge-off rate (goeasy)** | **16.7%** | 8.7% | **+800 bps** |
| Allowance for credit losses | $499.5M | $406.7M | +23% |
| Allowance rate (% of gross receivables) | **9.99%** | — | — |
| Gross consumer loans receivable | $5.00B | $5.11B | −2% |
| Net charge-offs (Propel Holdings) | **12%** of avg. balances | ~12% | **Stable** |

Two figures anchor everything below, and they pull against each other. goeasy's losses **roughly doubled**. Propel's **did not move**. Any reading of loan charge-off rates Canada wide has to account for both.

## The distinction that changes the interpretation

Loan charge-off rates Canada reports are frequently read as a live thermometer of household finances. They are not. **They are a lagging indicator, and the lag is long.**

The sequence behind a single charge-off runs roughly like this:

1. **Loan originated** — often 12 to 24 months before write-off.
2. **Payments missed** — delinquency begins.
3. **Delinquency deepens** — typically 90 to 180 days of non-payment.
4. **Charged off** — the lender stops expecting repayment.

A loan written off in Q2 2026 was, in most cases, **advanced in 2024 or early 2025** and went bad months before the write-off was booked. So a 16.7% rate is evidence about borrowing conditions and household stress **a year or more ago** — not about July 2026.

That resolves what would otherwise look like a contradiction. Canada's July jobs report showed employment up **75,000** and unemployment at **6.4%**, a two-year low. **Improving employment and rising charge-offs can coexist comfortably**, because they are measuring different moments in time.

## Why one lender doubled and another didn't

If deteriorating household finances alone drove loan charge-off rates Canada wide, both lenders would show it. They didn't — and that single fact should temper how far this figure is read as a national verdict.

goeasy identified specific pressure points, tightening underwriting on **merchant-originated automotive and powersports loans** and moderating direct-to-consumer originations. Propel's growth was concentrated in **Lending-as-a-Service** (revenue up 150%) and **the UK** (up 53%) — lines with different risk characteristics from Canadian direct consumer lending.

The reasonable conclusion is that **product mix and underwriting explain much of the gap**, not the economy alone. A rise this sharp at one lender should not be read as a national household-credit reading on its own.

## What an allowance rate of 9.99% signals

Loan charge-off rates Canada lenders publish look backwards. Beyond charge-offs already taken, goeasy's **allowance for credit losses** — money set aside against expected future losses — reached **$499.5 million**, or **9.99% of gross receivables**.

That is a forward-looking number, and it is arguably more informative than the backward-looking charge-off rate. Setting aside nearly a tenth of the loan book says the lender expects elevated losses to continue.

Its own guidance confirms it: **full-year 2026 net charge-offs of 14.5% to 16.0%** — well above last year's 8.7%, and treated as the operating assumption rather than a shock.

## Why this matters if you are borrowing or repaying

Rising loan charge-off rates Canada lenders report have four consequences for borrowers, and they pull in different directions.

1. **Approvals tighten.** A lender absorbing losses at this level lends less. goeasy cut originations **70%** in the quarter. Refinancing out of an existing debt is harder in that environment.
2. **Hardship options may improve.** Lenders recover more from a restructured loan than a written-off one. That makes them more receptive to payment arrangements — but only if you contact them **before** missing payments.
3. **The window before a charge-off is long — and useful.** Months typically pass between a first missed payment and a write-off. That entire period is negotiable. It is also the period most borrowers spend avoiding the lender's calls.
4. **Your credit file records the ending.** A charged-off debt sent to collections generally appears as an R9 and can remain for about six years, regardless of how the lender accounts for it internally.

## Loan charge-off rates Canada: the timeline that matters

If you are behind on a loan, the useful information is not the loss rate — it is how much time typically sits between a missed payment and a write-off, because every stage of it is negotiable.

| Stage | Typical timing | What you can still do |
|---|---|---|
| First missed payment | Day 1 | Call before this happens; ask for a deferral |
| 30 days past due | Month 1 | Hardship arrangements are most available here |
| 90 days past due | Month 3 | Restructuring still possible; collections looming |
| Assigned to collections | Months 3–6 | Negotiating position weakens; reporting begins |
| Charged off | Months 6–12+ | Debt persists; R9 on file for ~6 years |

![A repayment timeline showing the window before loan charge-off rates Canada are recorded](https://images.pexels.com/photos/7144207/pexels-photo-7144207.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

The gap between the first and last rows is often **six months to a year**. That entire window is time in which a lender would rather restructure than write off — and elevated loss rates make them *more* willing to talk, not less, because a recovering loan beats a charged-off one on their books.

## Key takeaways

- **goeasy's net charge-off rate hit 16.7%**, up from 8.7% — roughly double.
- **Allowance for credit losses reached $499.5M**, or **9.99%** of gross receivables.
- **Propel Holdings' charge-offs held stable at 12%** in the same quarter.
- **Charge-offs lag by 12–24 months**, so these reflect 2024–2025 conditions, not today's labour market.
- **Guidance of 14.5–16.0%** for full-year 2026 signals elevated losses are expected to persist.
- **The pre-charge-off window is long and negotiable** — act early.

## The bottom line

Loan charge-off rates Canada saw in Q2 2026 are genuinely elevated at one major lender, and its own forecasts expect that to continue through the year. But the figure is a rear-view mirror, not a windscreen: it describes loans made in a different rate and price environment, going bad on a long delay.

The more useful reading is what lenders do next. goeasy's answer was to cut new lending by 70% and suspend its dividend. For borrowers, that response — rather than the loss rate itself — is what will actually be felt at the application stage.
