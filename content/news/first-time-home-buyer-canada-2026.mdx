## First Time Home Buyer Canada 2026: The Down Payment Wall

For years the conversation was about interest rates. In 2026 the harder problem is the lump sum you need up front. CMHC's latest survey pegs the average time to save a down payment at **4.4 years** — and that figure blends in repeat buyers who often roll equity from a previous home into the next purchase. A true first-time buyer, starting from zero, usually faces a longer climb.

The longer arc explains why. Between 1981 and 2024, median real (inflation-adjusted) hourly wages grew about **20%**, while inflation-adjusted home prices climbed **163.5%**, according to CBC News. Pay crept up; prices ran away. That gap is the down payment wall in a single statistic.

And yet the appetite hasn't faded: **81%** of Canadians still believe homeownership is a good long-term investment, CMHC found. The dream is intact — the entry ticket just costs more time.

## Inside Nova Scotia's 2% Down Payment Pilot

Here is the development every first time home buyer Canada 2026 is talking about. Nova Scotia has become the **first province** to cut the minimum down payment for first-time buyers from the usual **5% to 2%**, under a **four-year pilot**. On a home where 5% down once meant a five-figure hurdle, a 2% floor can shave that requirement by more than half.

It is not a free pass. To qualify, buyers must:

- Have a household income of **$200,000 or less**
- Pass the **CMHC mortgage stress test**
- Have a credit score of at least **630**

The signal matters as much as the mechanics. A province is treating the down payment — not the mortgage rate — as the true barrier to entry, and testing whether lowering it helps first-time buyers get in the door. Whether other provinces follow will be one of the housing stories to watch across the pilot's four years.
