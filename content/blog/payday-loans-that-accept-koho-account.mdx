**Payday loans that accept KOHO** are not decided by the lender's opinion of you. They are decided, in almost every case, by whether a third-party bank-verification vendor has KOHO on its supported list. That is a technical coverage question with nothing to do with your income — and it explains why one lender approves in four minutes and the next declines instantly on the same file.

## Quick answer

Some do, some do not, and the list changes. A March 2026 review by Finder Canada listed **Money Mart** and **Loan Express** as accepting KOHO, and reported that **iCash confirmed it does not**. The stated reason is that **Flinks**, one of the verification platforms most commonly used by subprime lenders, may not currently support KOHO. Where that is the blocker, the workaround is **manual income verification** — slower, document-based, and entirely legitimate.

## Why payday loans that accept KOHO come down to one vendor

Most online lending decisions run through three separate systems, and only one of them is a judgement about you:

| Stage | What it does | Where KOHO can fail |
|---|---|---|
| **Verification** | Reads 90 days of transactions through a data connector | The connector may not list KOHO as a supported institution |
| **Funding** | Sends money, usually by Interac e-Transfer | Rarely a problem — e-Transfer runs on an email address |
| **Repayment** | Pre-authorized debit using institution, transit and account numbers | The institution number is **621, Peoples Trust** — not KOHO |

Notice that only the first and third stages are affected, and neither is about creditworthiness. Your income can be flawless and the file still stops at stage one because a vendor has not built a connector.

That is also why the honest answer to "which lenders accept KOHO?" is *ask today*. The answer is a coverage list maintained by companies you never deal with, and it is revised without announcements.

## Payday loans that accept KOHO and the Peoples Trust detail

KOHO is not, at present, a deposit-taking institution. Customer balances are held with **Peoples Trust Company**, a federally regulated institution, which is why your direct-deposit details show **institution number 621** and a Peoples Trust transit number rather than anything branded KOHO.

Practical consequences worth knowing before you apply:

- On any form asking for an institution *name*, write **Peoples Trust Company**, not KOHO. Mismatched names are a common rejection reason on pre-authorized debit setup.
- Some employer and government payroll systems validate the institution name against the number. Use the exact details from the KOHO app.
- If a lender tells you the account "isn't a bank account," this is what they mean. It is not a judgement of the account's reliability.

KOHO has been working through the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions' multi-year process toward a **Schedule 1 banking licence**. If and when that is granted, KOHO would hold deposits itself as a CDIC member with its own institution number — which is exactly the structural gap causing today's friction. In other words, the answer to this question has an expiry date.

## KOHO lends too — and that changes the question

Here is what most articles on this keyword miss entirely. KOHO is not only the account. It also offers **Cover**, a cash advance of up to **$250** with **no interest**, for a subscription starting around **$2 a month**, repaid automatically as money is added to the account. A line of credit is offered through a third-party lender.

So the real decision for a KOHO user is not "which payday lender takes my account." It is **which regulatory regime you want to be in**:

| | Licensed payday loan | KOHO Cover | Instalment loan / line of credit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maximum | $1,500 | **$250** | Typically $1,000+ |
| Cost on $250 | **$35** in fees | $0 interest + subscription | Interest at up to 35% APR |
| Cost if you do not borrow | $0 | **The subscription still runs** | $0 |
| Covered by the 14% cost cap | **Yes** | **No** — it is not a payday loan | No — capped at 35% APR instead |
| Two-day cancellation right | **Yes** (Ontario) | No | No |
| Mandatory extended payment plan | **Yes** (Ontario, 3rd loan in 63 days) | No | No |
| Collection contact limits | **Yes** | Not under payday rules | Not under payday rules |

Read that table twice. On $250, Cover is plainly cheaper in dollars. But cheaper is not the same as safer: because a subscription cash advance is not a licensed payday loan, none of the payday consumer protections attach to it. You are trading a price cap and a cancellation right for a lower fee. That can be the right trade — just make it deliberately.

## Five checks before you apply for payday loans that accept KOHO

1. **Ask the lender directly whether it supports KOHO**, by chat or phone, before you submit anything. A decline still leaves a footprint.
2. **Have the manual route ready.** Two recent pay stubs or a benefits statement, plus 90 days of KOHO statements as PDF. This is the standard fallback where instant verification fails.
3. **Copy the deposit details exactly** from the KOHO app — Peoples Trust Company, institution 621, transit, account.
4. **Confirm the lender is licensed in your province.** In Ontario, if an unlicensed lender writes you a payday loan, section 6(3) of the *Payday Loans Act, 2008* means you owe only the advance, not the cost of borrowing.
5. **Price the cheapest version of your problem first.** $250 through Cover, a bill deferral, or a 35% APR instalment loan will all beat $14 per $100 if you have a day to spare.

## If you were already declined, do this in order

A verification decline is not a credit decline, and treating it as one leads people to make it worse. Work through this sequence instead:

1. **Ask what actually failed.** Support agents can usually say whether the bank connection failed or the income assessment did. Those are entirely different problems and only one is about you.
2. **Do not immediately reapply elsewhere.** Three applications in an afternoon create a pattern of repeated enquiries and repeated verification attempts, which is itself a decline signal at the next lender.
3. **Request manual review at the same lender** before moving on. Most lenders that support **payday loans that accept KOHO** at all will accept documents where the connector fails — you are asking them to use a lane they already operate.
4. **Send the right three documents:** 90 days of KOHO statements exported as PDF, proof of income for the same period, and the exact deposit details from the app.
5. **Check whether the amount was the problem.** Ontario caps a single payday loan at 50% of net income. An application above that ceiling is declined regardless of which bank you use — asking for less often converts the same file.
6. **If it still fails, change the destination, not the lender.** A basic no-fee chequing account at a CDIC member institution takes minutes to open and removes the verification obstacle permanently, while KOHO keeps doing what it is good at.

That last option annoys people, and it should not. Using one account for lender-facing plumbing and another for day-to-day spending is ordinary financial hygiene, not a concession.

## The bottom line

Whether you can get **payday loans that accept KOHO** is a plumbing question, not a character question. A vendor's coverage list decides stage one, an institution number belonging to Peoples Trust decides stage three, and both are likely to change once KOHO holds deposits in its own right. In the meantime, ask before you apply, keep the manual-verification file ready, and remember that the cheapest $250 available to a KOHO user is usually not a payday loan at all — though it is also not covered by the rules that make payday loans predictable.
